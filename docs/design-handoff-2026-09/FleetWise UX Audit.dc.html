FleetWise — UI/UX audit Rapid Rise · 31 July 2026
FleetWise

What’s wrong with the interface, and what I changed

A screen-by-screen audit of the FleetWise web app, carried out by reading the source rather than screenshots. 176 findings across 22 screens — every route, dialog and role in the product — each with the fix now designed in FleetWise Redesign. Six are genuine code defects and should be fixed whether or not the redesign ships.

63 Critical — costs the user the task
66 Serious — slows or misleads
47 Polish — erodes trust

How to read this

Part 1 lists the code defects. Part 2 is the part worth reading if you only read one: nine mistakes that repeat across the whole app, where a single fix improves many screens at once. Part 3 is the full list, screen by screen. Part 4 records what deliberately did not change.

Nothing here asks for a schema change, a new table or a different server action. Two items need a small behavioural addition and are flagged as such.

Part 1 · Code defects

These are bugs, not opinions. Each was found by reading the source and can be confirmed in a few minutes.

BUG 1

An onboarding step marks itself done

In onboarding/page.tsx, step 1 (“add your machines”) and step 3 (“put QR stickers on them”) share the same condition — done: machines > 0. Adding a single machine ticks both.

Why it matters: QR stickers are how the whole no-login driver flow starts. Farms are being told they’ve done it when they haven’t, which plausibly explains low scan adoption. Step 3 needs its own condition — labels printed, or an explicit “I’ve done this” acknowledgement.

BUG 2

A bilingual app whose front door is English-only

Every t() call on the login page is made without a locale argument, because language lives on the user’s profile and no profile is loaded before sign-in. The same applies to the public QR pages.

Why it matters: the app is at EN/AF parity everywhere else, invites default to Afrikaans, and yet the first screen every Afrikaans farm sees is English with no toggle. Needs a device-level locale (cookie or Accept-Language) for pre-auth pages.

BUG 3

“Act into farm” doesn’t act into anything

impersonateFarm calls log_admin_farm_access, redirects with ?entered=1, and does nothing else. No farm context is set and no session state changes — but the flash reads “Support access logged”.

Why it matters: staff believe they are inside a customer account when they are not, and there is no banner or exit because there is no mode to exit. Either implement the farm-context cookie (and log an exit action so duration is recorded), or rename the button to what it does.

BUG 4

Deleting a machine had no confirmation step

The soft-delete fired directly from the button press on the machine detail page. Separately, the POPIA erasePerson action — which permanently anonymises a person and bans their login — was a variant="ghost" size="sm" button guarded only by a browser confirm().

Why it matters: the two most destructive actions in the product were the two easiest to trigger by accident. Both now have deliberate confirmation — the machine delete states what will be hidden, the person erasure requires typing the name.

BUG 5

Approving money takes one tap, with nothing in the way

acceptQuote and approveInvoice commit the farm to real money, and both fire straight from a size="sm" submit. On the same row, millimetres away, sit a machine-name link and three icon-only contact buttons.

Why it matters: a mis-tap on a phone approves a five-figure invoice, and there is no undo. Every other destructive action now has a confirmation; this one commits money and had none.

BUG 6

The import error message is two unrelated strings

In import-client.tsx, the CSV header failure renders {t("machines.err.name_required")} — {t("machines.previewTitle")}, producing “Name is required — Preview”.

Why it matters: wrong headers are the most likely way an import fails, and this is the entire feedback. It names a problem that isn’t the problem, then appends a heading. Needs its own message naming the columns we couldn’t find.

Part 2 · Nine mistakes that repeat everywhere

Many of the 176 findings are instances of these nine. A finding can belong to more than one pattern, so the counts below overlap and do not sum to 176. Fixing a pattern once — usually in a shared component — improves every screen that uses it.

1 · The database is showing through — 21 findings

Column names, enum values and raw numbers reach the screen untranslated: {current_reading} {meter_type} printing “184320 km”, vat_rate_bps asking a farmer for 1500, a Badge passed the raw u.role with a capitalize class, rendering “Rr_admin”, updated_at.slice(0,10) and toLocaleDateString("en-ZA") printing ISO dates in the contractor portal, the inbox and three times per traffic fine. Fix: a formatting layer between query and render — thousands separators, unit words, role labels, relative dates.

2 · Everything has equal weight — 14 findings

Nine cards on the dashboard, twenty on machine detail, eight report cards, eight settings cards — all the same size, all always open, in schema order. A machine standing broken looked like a fuel summary. Fix: one ranked “what needs you” block per screen; everything else demoted to tabs or a secondary column.

3 · Numbers with nothing to do — 12 findings

Counters that state a problem without offering the action, and links that go to the wrong place: tapping “2 Overdue” opened the reports page rather than the two overdue machines; fault rows linked to the fault list. Fix: every count deep-links to the things it counts, and carries the verb button beside it.

4 · Placeholder-only form fields — 10 findings

Ten inputs on the public QR page with no <label> at all; both login email boxes; the money fields in the contractor portal. The question disappears the moment the user types — worst for the users with the least confidence. Fix: persistent labels everywhere; the Field component already supports this.

5 · Submit-to-filter forms — 4 findings

Machines, work requests, parts and job cards each open with a card of dropdowns and a Search button, eating the first screen on a phone before any data appears — and in the contractor portal, chips that apply instantly sit beside dropdowns that don’t. Fix: filter chips that apply on tap, writing the same URL params; useful default ordering so most users never filter.

6 · Status carried by colour alone, or by no colour at all — 9 findings

The machines list rendered all six statuses as tone="neutral", so broken and working looked identical; elsewhere a person who left the farm got the same danger red as an overdue service. Fix: shape + word + colour on every status, and red reserved for destruction and genuine alarm.

7 · Server strings shown as user copy — 7 findings

Errors round-trip through the query string and land on screen verbatim — ?error=Invalid+login+credentials, ?error=Enter+a+valid+reading, Supabase rate-limit messages — visible both in the page and the address bar. ?error=forbidden isn’t rendered at all. Fix: map known codes to sentences that say what to do next.

8 · Photos treated as decoration — 7 findings

The schema does real work for images — primary_attachment_id, batch-signed URLs, a Storage bucket for fault photos — and it was spent on 36px and 64px thumbnails. The dashboard never used the photo at all. Fix: on mobile, the photo identifies the machine and leads the card; a driver’s fault photo is the most useful object in the record.

9 · Destructive and dangerous actions styled quietly — 14 findings

Delete with no confirm; POPIA erasure as a ghost link; entering a customer’s farm as a small secondary button; deleteFine as a 12px red text link on a record with a legal deadline; acceptQuote and approveInvoice committing five figures from a size="sm" submit; and inversely, Add a part styled primary while Save the job card was secondary. Visual weight did not track consequence. Fix: one primary action per screen, matched to the real decision; destructive actions confirmed in proportion to what they destroy.

Part 3 · Screen by screen

Each finding is tagged C critical, S serious or P polish. The designed fix for each is in FleetWise Redesign, under the matching screen number.

S01 · Farm home /dashboard

Owner and farm manager. The screen most people open first every morning.

  • C Seven counters before one instruction. Overdue, due soon, open faults, in workshop, two spend figures and services-OK — the owner had to work out what to do. Replaced by one ranked list with the action attached.
  • C Every KPI tile linked to /reports. “2 Overdue” opened a report, not the two machines. Fault rows linked to the fault list, never the fault.
  • S Nine equal cards, nine scroll-screens. Cut to four blocks; breakdowns moved to Costs & reports.
  • S Empty states said “None”. Some cards used the good EmptyState, others a bare grey dash. Empty is the good outcome here — now “Nothing needs you today”.
  • S The page was titled “Dashboard”. Jargon, and it told a multi-farm user nothing about which farm they were in.
  • P “Danie Kruger · owner” in 12px grey at the bottom. Debug output shipped as UI, in the most-scrolled-to slot on the page.
  • P 14 nav items, 11 hidden in “More”. The daily action — report a problem — was nowhere in the chrome.
  • P Machines were names, not machines. A driver recognises the green John Deere long before he reads a serial number.

S02 · Machines list /machines

  • C Every status was the same grey. All six states rendered tone="neutral" — a broken machine and a working one were visually identical rows.
  • C A filter form you had to submit. Five dropdowns and a Search button consumed the entire first screen on a phone, and nothing happened until submit.
  • C Raw meter values. “184320 km”, “6412 hours” — no separators, database column as the unit, and no indication the reading was a month old.
  • S The photo was 36 pixels. Signed-URL batching spent on a thumbnail too small to recognise.
  • S “—” in pale grey for no service plan. An invisible dash hiding a real to-do — and the farm’s adoption target.
  • S No count, no result feedback. Nothing said how many machines existed or that a filter was hiding some.
  • P One empty state for two different problems. “No machines” read the same whether the farm had none or a filter matched none — and offered “Add machine” when the fix was clearing the filter.
  • P A desktop table with nothing to do. Seven read-only columns; logging an hour reading meant opening the machine and losing your place.

S03 · Job card /jobcards/[id]

  • C Eight identical grey boxes, four of them jargon. Diagnosis, work performed, recommendations — all rows={2}, all equal, on a phone in a workshop. Now three plain questions.
  • C Two submit buttons, and the wrong one was primary. Add a part was primary while Save the job card was secondary.
  • C It autosaved every 500ms and never said so. Good engineering, invisible — while a Save button implied the opposite.
  • S You could only discover the blocker by being blocked. “Mark completed” opened a modal, then disabled its own confirm button. The requirement is now visible before you try.
  • S Status was a dropdown of database values. Five states hidden in a select — you had to open it to learn where the job stood.
  • S VAT explained in arrow notation. R968.30 incl → R842.00 ex + R126.30 vat, with a 16px checkbox.
  • P No running total while you worked. The number the owner will ask about only appeared on the list page.
  • P The recovered-draft banner looked like an error. Amber warning styling with 24px-tall Restore / Discard buttons, at the one moment the app is saving your work.

S04 · Machine detail /machines/[id]

  • C Twenty cards, one scroll, no hierarchy. 1,400 lines rendering meter history, fuel, service plan, kit, compliance, budget, timeline and more, all at once.
  • C Delete had no confirm at all. See Bug 4.
  • C The graph vanished when you needed it most. if (pts.length < 2) return null — a new machine, or one nobody is reading, shows nothing at all rather than saying so.
  • S Graph axis labels at 12px in sand-400. The caption carried the only readable data in the chart.
  • S The red alert card was one of twenty. A machine standing broken got the same footprint as a fuel summary.
  • S Jargon headings straight from the spec. “Utilisation”, “Lifetime stats”, “Compliance”, “Watch items”, “Service kit”, “Meter history”.
  • P The machine’s photo wasn’t the hero on a phone. On the one screen where you stand beside the machine, the photo was a thumbnail among cards.
  • P Success was a query string. ?saved=reading, ?error=Enter+a+valid+reading — server-worded feedback in the URL bar.

S05 · Scan the sticker · report a problem /m/[token], /faults

The no-login flow. The only screen many workers will ever use.

  • C Every field was placeholder-only — no labels at all. Ten inputs on the public page, not one <label>, for the least confident users in the product.
  • C It never showed you which machine you scanned. Just the name as text — no photo, no confirmation, though stickers get swapped and codes get scanned from a distance.
  • C The written description came before the voice note and camera. The two inputs that need no literacy sat below the text box as afterthoughts.
  • S Five ghost buttons and a dropdown, all the same weight. Acknowledge, Start work, To job card, assignee select + Assign, Resolve — every one ghost size="sm". When everything is quiet, nothing is obvious.
  • S Sorted alphabetically by status, not by how bad it is. .order("status") put “acknowledged” above “open”, so a dead machine could sit under a torn seat.
  • S The photo of the broken part was 64 pixels. h-16 w-16, and the voice note was a bare <audio controls>.
  • P Emoji doing the work of icons. A tractor emoji as the logo, a fuel pump in a heading, a pin for the map link — rendering differently on every Android in the district.
  • P “No faults” looked the same as a broken page. An empty fault list is the best news on the farm and got the generic empty state.

S06 · Contractor portal /contractor, /work/[id]

  • C The eight-step stepper, as tiny scrolling pills. Eight text-xs chips in an overflow-x-auto row — on a phone you saw three and had to swipe.
  • C Two competing ways to change status. Contextual quick buttons beside a dropdown of all eight values, including ones that move the job backwards.
  • C Money entered in a 112px box labelled “R”. Quote and invoice both used a bare w-28 input with a 16px VAT checkbox — the number the business runs on.
  • S Work grouped by database lifecycle value. Eight possible groups in enum order; the contractor’s real question is “what must I do today”.
  • S R14,200 — but is that a quote or a bill? The word carrying the meaning was text-xs text-sand-400 after the biggest number on the card.
  • S Another submit-to-filter form. Kind chips applied instantly; status, client and sort sat behind a Search button — two interaction models on one card.
  • P Raw ISO dates everywhere. updated_at.slice(0,10) when what matters is how long the farmer has been waiting.
  • P The Pro upsell was a dashed grey box. A lock line and “contact Rapid Rise” in 12px grey — no price, no way to act.

S07 · Costs & reports, Diesel /reports, /fuel

  • C Two forms, fourteen inputs, side by side. “Log draw” and “Log fill” both open at once — a wall of boxes for someone standing at a pump.
  • C Four words for two ideas. Purchased · Delivered · Attributed · Issued — bookkeeping language for “came in” and “went into machines”.
  • C The tank balance was the last line on the page. The number that catches diesel theft, as a small text row under both forms.
  • S A whole anomaly engine, shown as a hover tooltip. Migration 0242 computes consumption anomalies properly; the UI drew 16 bars min-w-[3px] whose only explanation was a title attribute — invisible on touch.
  • S Eight report cards, all open, all equal. Reports answer a question; this was one long scroll of same-sized cards.
  • S Nine separate CSV links, scattered. Each a small link on its own card, plus an audit-pack PDF elsewhere.
  • P “R126 per hour” with nothing to compare it to. A number only means something next to another number.
  • P The SARS rebate was never mentioned. The activity dropdown exists so a farm can claim the diesel refund, and nothing said so — which is why drivers left it blank.

S08 · People, Farm settings /team, /settings

  • C Permanent erasure as a small grey link. See Bug 4.
  • C VAT entered in basis points. The field was vat_rate_bps, default 1500. A farmer typing 15 silently sets VAT to 0.15%.
  • C Seventeen numbers, no idea what any of them do. Due-soon hours, stale days, anomaly %, repair-replace %, utilisation hours — a label and nothing else.
  • S Roles printed straight from the database. “Rr_admin”, “Workshop” — and nothing explained the difference between a mechanic and an operator, which is the choice you make when inviting someone.
  • S “Active: No” in danger red. A person who left the farm is not an error, but got the same red as an overdue service.
  • S Sorted alphabetically by role enum. .order("role") put manager, mechanic and operator above owner, because m precedes o.
  • P One Save button, eight cards down. Change the first field, scroll past seven cards to save, with no unsaved indication on the way.
  • P Two big switches as 20px checkboxes. “Approval required” changes every job card on the farm; “cost visible to operators” decides who sees money.

S09 · Login, first-run onboarding /login, /onboarding

  • C Two email boxes on one small screen. A password form and a magic-link form, each with its own name="email". Autofill fills the first, the user taps the second button, nothing happens.
  • C A bilingual app with an English-only front door. See Bug 2.
  • C A setup step that ticks itself. See Bug 1.
  • S Raw Supabase errors, in the URL bar. “Invalid login credentials”, “Email rate limit exceeded” — first contact with the product, in developer English.
  • S “Check your email” was a green strip above the same form. ?sent=1 left both forms sitting there, so people typed their address again.
  • S Nothing for the people with no email. Much of the workforce uses the QR stickers and needs no login — the login screen never said so, so drivers got stuck at the door.
  • P Placeholder-only fields, again. On the most anxious screen in the product.
  • P Finished steps kept shouting. A completed step stayed full size at opacity-80 with its button intact, so the remaining task had no more presence than the done ones.

S10 · Rapid Rise admin console /admin/farms

  • C “Act into farm” doesn’t act into anything. See Bug 3.
  • C Entering a customer’s farm was a small secondary button. Reading a farmer’s private data, with no confirmation and no reason captured for the log.
  • S The churn radar was the last column. Scope calls this table a churn radar; “last activity” was rightmost, red only after 14 days, and the list sorted by signup date.
  • S A create form permanently occupying the top of the page. Done a few times a month, above 31 farms on every visit.
  • S Money shown three ways, hedged in 12px grey. The crucial caveat — no charge is made — was the smallest text on the card.
  • P Two forms and a Save button per farm. Three fields and a submit for what is usually one change.
  • P The access log said “admin · impersonate”. Enum values and locale timestamps, in a log a customer may one day demand to see.
  • P Nothing marked this as the internal tool. The same green farmer chrome, distinguished only by a 12px grey label — a screen-share couldn’t tell staff view from customer view.

S11 · Driver / operator home new screen

The operator role has no screen of its own today.

  • C Denied users are dumped on the owner’s dashboard. requireRole redirects every role to /dashboard?error=forbidden — a driver lands on a page of Rands the farm can explicitly switch off for operators.
  • C “forbidden” is never rendered. The dashboard has no flash for it, so the screen simply changes with no explanation.
  • C The hardest user got the generic shell. The same sidebar, tabs and “More” sheet built for fourteen destinations, handed to someone with four tasks.
  • S No way to pick a machine without reading. Every chooser is a select of names and serials; a grid of photos needs no literacy.
  • S Nothing told them their report landed. A driver reports a fault and never hears anything again, so next time he tells the foreman instead.
  • S Their language setting was never used on their screens. Invites default to Afrikaans, but the driver’s journey is exactly where the locale is missing.
  • P Scan was buried, though it’s the whole point. No scan entry existed in the signed-in app at all.
  • P Sign out hidden two taps deep. On a shared bakkie phone, signing out matters more than for anyone else.

S12 · Owner inbox /inbox

Where the farm commits to spending money with outside workshops.

  • C Approving money had no confirmation. See Bug 5.
  • C The approve button sat beside a link, on a phone. Machine-name link, three icon-only contact buttons and the approve submit in one flex row — five targets in a few millimetres, the largest of which spends money.
  • C You could only say yes. Declining a price, or querying a bill that doesn’t match its quote, had no path — so those conversations happen on WhatsApp and the system loses them.
  • S Four counters splitting two facts. Outstanding quotes · quote value · outstanding invoices · invoice value — count and money as separate tiles, to be paired mentally.
  • S A bill for finished work looked like a quote. One is “may they start”, the other is “the work is done, pay them” — distinguished only by a badge and the button’s wording.
  • S Contact was three unlabelled icons. Phone, WhatsApp and mail as ghost size="sm" glyphs with only an aria-label.
  • P Active work nested three levels deep. Six pieces of metadata per line at 12–14px, indented under a machine heading.
  • P Read state needed a button press per item. Each notification carried its own 20px-tall form, with toLocaleDateString("en-ZA") beneath.

S13 · Traffic fines & AARTO nominations /fines

The screen with a statutory deadline attached to it.

  • C A legal deadline in 12px grey. Miss the nomination date and the fine plus demerit points stay with the farm. It rendered as a raw ISO date in text-xs text-sand-500, third line down.
  • C The clever part was hidden in a disclosure triangle. Naming the driver — the point of the screen, backed by a real usage-log lookup — sat behind a <details> summary styled text-xs.
  • C Deleting a fine was a 12px red text link. deleteFine with px-1 padding and no confirmation, on a record with a legal deadline attached.
  • S Twelve fields to log one fine — behind a separate GET form you had to submit first.
  • S The suggestion never said why. “Suggested: Thabo Mokoena” in a strip — the evidence (he logged diesel into that bakkie that day, at that reading) was fetched and thrown away.
  • S A status dropdown and a Set button on every card. Two interactions to record “the driver paid it”.
  • P Three ISO dates per fine. Offence date, fine date and deadline, in a run of dot-separated fragments.
  • P No per-driver pattern anywhere. The data supports it and repeat offenders are exactly what an owner wants to see.

S14 · Daily checks /checklists

  • C You cannot actually do a check. The page lists template definitions with Edit / Duplicate / Delete. There is no “start a check”, no history, and nothing linking a template to a machine — the admin screen exists, the daily habit it supports does not.
  • C A chevron that does nothing. When a row isn’t editable the actions are replaced by a right-arrow inside a plain span — not a link, goes nowhere, reads as “tap for more”.
  • C Delete a template with one click, no confirm. A bare red-text button beside Edit and Duplicate.
  • S “Global” and “Farm” as badges. Multi-tenancy vocabulary on a farmer’s screen.
  • S “6 fields · updated 2026-07-14”. Field count is a schema fact. What matters is who did it last and what it found — both available, neither shown.
  • S Edit, Duplicate and Delete at equal weight. Three actions per row, one destructive, none of them the thing anyone came to do.
  • P The driver can’t reach it. Operators are correctly excluded from editing templates, but they are the people who should be doing the checks, and no operator-facing route exists.
  • P Nothing said what a check is for. A driver won’t report a worn belt if he thinks it lands him in trouble.

S15 · Parts catalogue /parts

  • C A form inside a disclosure inside a table cell. Editing a part opens a <details> nested in the part-number cell, revealing five unlabelled fixed-width inputs wrapping inside one column. Unusable on a phone.
  • C Placeholder-as-label, again. The inline edit row drops the Field component entirely for raw placeholder-only inputs.
  • C Delete as 12px red text, no confirm. Deleting a part that job cards reference is not a small act.
  • S “Add a part” hidden behind a disclosure. The primary reason to visit the page was a summary you had to click first.
  • S Another submit-to-filter search, on a list of 184 rows where filtering is the main activity.
  • S “Typical cost” — typical according to whom? A hand-typed number that goes stale immediately, while every job card records what was actually paid.
  • P Four columns of “—”. A half-filled catalogue reads as a field of dashes.
  • P “Scope: Global / Farm” as a column. A whole column spent on vocabulary that only matters when you try to edit a row.

S16 · Alerts & notification preferences /notifications

  • C Settings above the content. You tap the bell to see what happened and get a preferences form; the notifications start below the fold.
  • C Quiet hours as two 0–23 number boxes, labelled Start and End with placeholder “inherit”. A farmer must work out that 20 means eight at night.
  • C The notification wasn’t tappable. No link to the thing it is about, though notificationUrl() exists and the inbox uses it. You are told a machine broke and cannot get to it.
  • S A 20px-tall “read” button per row, on the row you are already touching.
  • S Read state as opacity-75. Fading the card drops the text to roughly 3:1 contrast — below the accessible minimum, in bright sun.
  • S Sixty rows, no grouping, no icons. A breakdown and “hours are stale” look identical.
  • P Dates as toLocaleDateString("en-ZA") in the palest text in the design system, carrying the only ordering cue.
  • P “Mark all read” as a ghost button that only renders when something is unread, so the control moves between visits.

S17 · Partner directory /partners

  • C A login credential printed on the page. A freshly-minted contractor login URL renders as copyable plain text at the top, with no warning, and stays until the next navigation — visible to anyone passing the laptop or watching a screen-share. Treat as a security review item.
  • C Disclosures inside disclosures. Each card can hide an Invite and an Edit <details>; add-a-partner is in a third; the suggested list repeats the whole edit form again. Six near-identical collapsed forms on one page.
  • C Delete as 12px red text, no confirm — twice, in both the “yours” and “suggested” panels.
  • S “Connected” / “Not connected” as badges. Two words deciding whether a workshop can quote you inside FleetWise or must phone, neither explaining itself.
  • S “Suggested” never says by whom or why. Is it advertising? Vetted? Nearby?
  • S “No contact details” in the palest grey. A partner with no phone number is useless — a to-do, not a footnote.
  • P Contact buttons that appear and vanish. Call / WhatsApp / Email each render only if filled, so the row is a different width and order on every card.
  • P Nothing said who you actually use, though the cost ledger knows the spend with each.

S18 · Adding a machine /machines/new

  • C The photo came last, under a heading in the smallest palest style on the page — for the one field that makes every later screen recognisable.
  • C One long form, no save until the end. Thirteen-plus fields in a single form with one Save at the bottom. Lose signal in the shed and it is all gone.
  • S The button said “Save” — the generic string, on the button that creates a machine.
  • S Machine type as a dropdown of enum values. Type decides hours vs kilometres — a consequential choice with the consequence never stated.
  • S No guidance on the one field that matters. The name appears on every list, job card and fault report, and got no more prominence than “department”.
  • P The back link was 14px grey with a tiny chevron — well under 44px, and the only way out of a form with no cancel.
  • P Operator list loads every active user — owner, RR admins and contractors included — for a field usually left blank at creation.
  • P Errors arrive as a query string and render at the top — off-screen after a long scroll, with the failing field unmarked.

S19 · Work / job-card list /jobcards

  • C Six statuses, three colours. statusTone collapses reported, open and in_progress into one blue — the three states a mechanic moves between daily are visually identical.
  • C Creating a job card is a dropdown and an instant commit. Pick a machine, press New, and a record exists with type="repair" hardcoded — every job is a “repair”, and a mis-tap creates a record you must delete.
  • C You can’t see whose job it is. The query omits assignee and contractor, so the list cannot answer “what is mine” or “who is sitting on this”.
  • S Submit-to-filter, for the fourth time. Two selects and a Search button above the list.
  • S Raw date_in, and “—” when absent. What a workshop needs is how long it has been standing.
  • S No total, no grouping, no ordering by urgency. A machine standing broken sits below a routine service booked yesterday.
  • P The machine photo, unused again — a text name falling back to a literal “—”.
  • P “Job cards” is not a farm word, used as a top-level nav item for owners and drivers too.

S20 · Spreadsheet import /machines/import

  • C The header error is two unrelated strings. See Bug 6.
  • C No column mapping — your sheet must match ours exactly. Headers are validated against a fixed set, so a real farm spreadsheet with Afrikaans headings or a different column order fails wholesale. Every farm already has a machine list; making them retype it is why import doesn’t get used.
  • C Broken rows can’t be fixed, only re-uploaded. To correct one typo you edit the sheet in another program and start again.
  • S Errors as stacked badges of translated codes. Three problems means three red pills in a table cell, none saying what to do.
  • S Every row shown, valid or not. A 200-row sheet renders 200 preview rows with the three problems buried in the middle.
  • S A disabled button as the row-limit message. A farm with 300 machines is told “too many rows” and left there — no offer to split or load the first batch.
  • P The file picker is a dashed label with a hidden input — no drag-and-drop, and it reads as a placeholder rather than a button.
  • P No sense of where you are. Cards appear and disappear as state changes, with no step indicator and no way back to the chosen file.

S21 · RR service template library /admin/templates

  • C Service schedules typed as pipe-delimited text. Engine oil + filter | 250 | 12 in a four-row textarea, parsed on submit and re-serialised for editing. A stray pipe silently changes an interval for every farm using the template.
  • C No blast radius shown. These are global templates — editing one changes when machines fall due across the whole platform, and nothing said how many farms were affected.
  • C “Delete template” — plain text, no confirm, removing a schedule hundreds of machines may depend on.
  • S The whole editor hidden in “Edit (6 lines)”. The list shows a name and a type badge, nothing else.
  • S The format is explained once, in a paragraph under the page title. Scroll past it and every textarea is unlabelled.
  • S No usage data on a curation screen. Staff can’t tell which templates farms apply and which are dead weight.
  • P A create form permanently above the list — the same fault as the admin farms page.
  • P Farmer chrome on a staff tool, with no marker that edits here reach every farm.

S22 · Checklist question builder /checklists/new, /checklists/[id]/edit

  • C No preview of what the driver will see. A manager writes questions in grey boxes and never sees the phone screen a driver answers on — reading level, order and length are all invisible while you build it.
  • C Reordering by “Move up” / “Move down”. Two ~24px buttons per field; moving question eight to the top is seven round-trips.
  • C “Remove field” deletes instantly, in red text — the smallest target on the card is the destructive one.
  • S Every field shows every control at once. Four to five controls per question, all expanded — fifty controls on a ten-question form.
  • S Validation only fires on save, as one line at the bottom with no indication which field failed.
  • S Field types named after data types — “Checkbox”, “Rating”, “Section break” — the vocabulary of a form builder, not of someone deciding what to ask a driver.
  • P Labels typed straight onto inputs. The builder uses a local inputCls string rather than the design system’s Field and Input, so this screen drifts from every other form.
  • P No sense of how long the check will take. Nothing stops a manager writing twenty questions, which guarantees the check gets skipped.

Part 4 · What did not change

The brief was to upgrade the interface without altering how the system works. This is the record of that boundary.

Untouched

Every server action keeps its name, its fields and its validation. Every RLS policy, role gate and entitlement check is unchanged — including the belt-and-braces workshop_id filter, the token-gated public route with no anon DB access, and the guarded erase_personal_data RPC. All nine CSV routes, the workbook and the audit-pack PDF keep their URLs. Offline queueing, idempotency UUIDs and the job-card autosave draft key are as they were. VAT still stores basis points; the job-card total is still owned by the database trigger. Filter chips write the same URL params the old forms did. All eight work-request statuses and all five job-card statuses still exist and remain reachable.

Two places that need a small behavioural change

1 · Impersonation state. The support-mode banner cannot exist without a farm-context cookie set on enter and cleared on leave, plus a second log_admin_farm_access(…, 'exit') call so the log shows duration. Flagged rather than assumed.

2 · Where an operator lands. A role branch on the post-login redirect and on requireRole’s fallback — one line each, no new permissions.

Where the visual language came from

Colour is taken unchanged from the repo’s own tailwind.config.ts — the brand green ramp, the warm sand neutrals and the three traffic-light status tokens. What changed is typography (one family, Instrument Sans, with real tabular figures so hour meters and Rands align in columns), density, and the consistent use of 48px touch targets with an icon and a word on every action.
